My craft is orchestration: setting up people and AI agents so that everyone does what they do best, and someone sets the pace.

How my AI team works

  1. 01

    A human decides.

    Agents prepare, humans decide. No agent talks to customers.

  2. 02

    Every task has exactly one role.

    Orchestration hands each task to exactly one agent, with a role description, rules and memory.

  3. 03

    Whoever creates doesn’t review.

    Important drafts go through a separate review role before a human sees them.

  4. 04

    Every morning, five decisions at most.

    An oversight role bundles what’s open and presents it with a finished draft.

  5. 05

    Every fact has a source.

    Sensitive data passes through a gateway. Whatever is missing gets asked for, not made up.

What I’ve learned

I lead a team of AI agents myself. The mistakes your teams are making right now, I’ve already made.

  1. Agents make things up when the briefing demands it.

    RuleAsk for “a real example” without supplying one, and you’ll get one invented. Every briefing needs a truth condition.

  2. A gap quickly turns into a claim.

    Rule“There’s nothing on this in the data” becomes “this doesn’t exist”. Something missing is a follow-up question, not a result.

  3. Under time pressure, rubber-stamping wins.

    RuleWhoever creates doesn’t review. Reviewing is a role of its own.

  4. Polishing doesn’t make texts better.

    RuleEvery extra round sounded more like a machine. From the third round without new content, a human decides.

  5. In the end, the bottleneck is the human who has to decide.

    RuleDecisions need a fixed place and a fixed rhythm.
